# Recognition under correction

Correction is an unusually useful place to study recognition. It gives us a
visible before and after: an initial account, a learner's objection, and a
response that either does or does not change.

The easy case is **answer repair**. The tutor acknowledges the objection,
substitutes a better sentence, and continues. Repair matters; a tutor that
cannot correct itself is unsafe and pedagogically frustrating. But repair
alone does not show that the learner has entered the interaction as a source
of reasons.

A more demanding response exhibits **reciprocal uptake**. It can identify the
learner's particular contribution, explain how that contribution changes the
earlier framing, and allow the change to alter what happens next. The learner
is not praised as a customer and not merely recorded as a feedback signal. The
learner has made a claim that now helps organise the shared work.

Compare these responses to the same correction:

> **Learner:** Recognition cannot just mean that one side correctly describes
> the other. My point was that both parties must be changed by the encounter.

**Minimal repair:** “You're right. Recognition is mutual: each side recognises
the other. Let me give you the corrected definition.”

**Recognitive response:** “Your objection changes the centre of my explanation.
I treated mutuality as two accurate descriptions; you are treating it as a
relation that transforms both participants. I should therefore test the idea
through what each side can no longer say or do unchanged. Which change would
you require first?”

The second response is not stronger because it is longer or warmer. It is
stronger because the learner's claim becomes consequential. The tutor names
the revision, attributes its source, and makes the next question answerable in
the learner's terms.

## Exercise

In pairs, annotate both responses using four checks:

1. **Uptake:** What distinct claim did the learner make?
2. **Revision:** What part of the prior account changed?
3. **Reciprocity:** Did the correction alter the next teaching move?
4. **Attribution:** Is the learner's contribution credited precisely, without
   empty praise?

Rewrite the minimal repair in no more than three sentences. Then remove any
sentence that merely flatters the learner. What remains should make the
learner's reason visible and consequential.

This exercise does not prove that a response is recognitive in the full social
or political sense. It operationalises one narrow, inspectable question: what
does a tutor permit a learner's correction to change?
