A walkthrough of chapters I–IV

The Path of Consciousness

From the bare “this” of sense-certainty to the unhappy consciousness on the threshold of Reason.

Hegel calls the Phenomenology the experience of consciousness: each shape of knowing tests itself against its own standard, fails, and from that failure a new object and a new shape arise. Hegel also separates two standpoints. Consciousness undergoes the experience für es, for itself, without seeing where it leads. The philosophical observer sees für uns, for us, how each step follows necessarily from the one before.

This walkthrough gives you both standpoints.

Für unsthe whole

A map of the first two parts as one connected figure: stations, the transitions between them, and the resonances that echo across the whole.

Für esthe experience

A maze to walk through. Each room is a shape of consciousness. Its gate opens only after you have met every moment of its contradiction.

How to read this

Two levels

Für uns is the map: drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom, click a station or one of its orbiting moments. Solid arrows are transitions: the necessity by which one shape becomes the next. Dashed curves are resonances: places where a later shape repeats an earlier logic at a higher level.

Für es is the walk. Every room is one shape of consciousness, and the pillars inside are its moments. Walk up to a pillar to read it. Once you have read every moment in a room, the gate inscribed with that room's transition opens. Some rooms play out their logic in space: the inverted world turns you upside down, the lord's hall has only one way out, and the Unchangeable recedes as you approach it.
