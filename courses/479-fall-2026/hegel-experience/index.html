The interaction is a teaching analogy grounded in Pinkard’s translation: Sense-certainty §§90–110 · Perception §§111–131 · Understanding §§132 onward. The cup is our example, not Hegel’s.

The course question

What would the machine have to learn?

The three moments also describe increasingly rich functions a system might perform. But functional sophistication and lived experience are not the same claim.

Immediate signal

A sensor reads 64°C.

It registers one present value. Useful—but the reading alone has no history and does not decide what counts as a good cup.

Perceived thing

A classifier labels the cup.

“Coffee, warm, dark, low sugar.” It organizes properties into an object, but its categories and boundaries may remain fixed by designers.

Relational model

An adaptive system changes the recipe.

It predicts how beans, heat, milk, sugar, time, and your reactions interact—and revises future preparation from the outcome.

The Hegelian pressure is not only “did the output improve?” Ask whether the encounter became part of the system’s history, changed its own standard of a good cup, and changed what it treated as the relevant object.
