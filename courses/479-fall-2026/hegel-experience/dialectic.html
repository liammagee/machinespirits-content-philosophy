EPOL 479 · Reading Lab

The Experience of Consciousness

Hegel, Phenomenology of Spirit · open the reading (Pinkard tr.)

Five words you need — and one parable
☾ the night-watch
The italic interludes marked ☾ are our parable, not Hegel’s text: one watcher’s night, retold alongside the argument, losing what each scene loses. It mirrors; it never argues. Where it and Hegel differ, trust Hegel.
universal
What holds itself steady across different instances — “now”, “here”, “I”, “thing”, “red”. The opposite of a bare singular. Sense-certainty’s whole drama is discovering that its words were universals all along.
sublate (aufheben)
Hegel’s verb for how a shape fails: it is cancelled and kept. The wreckage is never thrown away — it becomes material for the next object.
in-itself / for-consciousness
What the object supposedly is independently of us, versus how it shows up for a knower. Experience is the discovery that the “in-itself” was an in-itself for consciousness — and the promotion of that discovery into a new object.
“for us”
Hegel’s observers — you, here. Consciousness never sees why its new object had to arrive; we watch the necessity from the balcony. (¶87: it happens “behind its back”.)
shape of consciousness
A whole stance toward the world: an object, a criterion of truth, and a way of failing built in. Sense-certainty, perception, and understanding are the first three.
