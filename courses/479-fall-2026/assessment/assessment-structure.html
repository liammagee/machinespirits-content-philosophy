Week 1 & 2:

Write an initial reponse (2-300 words; can be longer, not more than 500) to the following prompts. Use your own words here. Note this is not assessed.

The term “machine learning” connotes an obvious association with human learning. But it also implies differences. Write a response to the first prompt:

  • How would you define machine learning, in your own words and based on your own understanding of the term, today?
  • Next, how would you define human learning?
  • What are the similarities between the two? What are the differences?
  • What can we say about the relationship between the two?, In what ways does machine learning build upon human learning? And in what ways can human learning benefit - or not – from machine learning?
  • Finally, what is the most important question you want to pose and have answered about AI in relation to learning and education? Or asked a different way: what are you yourself hoping to learn about machine learning, human learning and the relationship between the two? And how would you answer this question provisionally?

Now this sounds like a survey, and in a way it is. But more importantly it acts as a certain baseline of your own understanding that will hopefully develop over the course. Each week we’ll revisit this understanding in the light of new concepts. This revisiting will form the spine of the course in terms of assessment.

Because each of our understandings is - and ought to be - different, I’ll be working with the idea of self-assessment. Each week you’ll be asked to reflect upon four activities: readings, lecture, group discussion and a chat session with your preferred AI system. Then you’ll be asked to revise your responses to these questions. Then you will also write a short reflective note and grade your weekly contribution.

  • Notes from readings, lecture, discussion
  • Chat with AI
  • Copy then Revise / Extend your Responses
  • Peer review in Class
  • Conclude with a short reflective note and self-assess

Each week:

Hour 1: Group discussion - previous week’s responses (30 minutes). Reflective note and self-assessment. Hour 2: Lecture (with interruptions please!) Hour 3: Group discussion (rotated): Discuss readings, lecture in relation to the week’s “concept” (experience, recognition etc).

10 minute breaks in between.

Assessment Structure:

Formative - self-assessed - 6 x 10% = 60% (concepts for weeks 2-7)

Peer Commentary: Weeks 3/4 & Weeks 6/7. 2 x 5% = 10% (hurdle requrement - not assessed).

Summative - synthesize materials into final course report /essay / curriculum design. We’ll discuss this later in the course, but it will definitely build upon the formative Assessments. 30%

You can think of the whole course as involving a series of six + one (6 + 1) iterations on your initial impulses, with a mix of self-and-other assessment.

I’ll also provide commentary at two points prior to the final assesment - weeks 3/4 and 6/7.

Comments on self-assessment

  • Subjective (obviously). But I would argue on two grounds:
    • As we shall see, Hegel is really the (Western) philosopher of self-assessment. In a Hegelian sense, I can only judge the facade of your learning - its outward appearance. Only you know if you’ve learned something - and I’ve tried to align assessment of the course with its content (as we’ll see, that is a Hegelian idea).
    • But also: in an age of AI we want to press learning to become more - not less - accountable.
  • Self-criticism - I reserve the right to upgrade marks that seem excessively punitive!
  • Unclear rubric. For the self-assessment tasks, I would suggest grading based on a calculus idea of a derivative. What did I know last week? What do I know now? How would I grade this rate of change?
