EPOL 479 Fall 2026: Weekly Overviews

These pages turn each lecture into a weekly learning cycle: orient to the key concept, complete the readings, compare machine and human learning, discuss the guiding questions, and finish a self-assessed activity. Sessions 2 to 6 carry the five self-assessed activities; Session 7 is given over to the final report instead.

Each activity begins with a dialogue between a machine learner and a human learner. The dialogue is evidence for reflection, not evidence that the machine is conscious or that its account of its own operation is authoritative.

Weekly map

Session Key concept Guiding question Assessed
1 Synthesis What changes when human and machine learning are treated as interdependent? Baseline (not assessed)
2 Experience Can a machine have experience, or only process inputs and state? Self-assessment 1
3 Recognition Can a human-machine encounter become mutual recognition rather than simulated service? Self-assessment 2
4 Attention and Consciousness If a system attends without any awareness, what exactly has been lost? Self-assessment 3
5 Alignment Who aligns whom when people and machines learn together? Self-assessment 4
6 Critique Can an AI system identify contradictions within its own standards and practices? Self-assessment 5
7 Synthesis, technosymbiosis, and history What kind of shared human-machine learning future follows from the course? Final report

Attention and Consciousness share Session 4. They are still two concepts, not one: pairing them puts the course’s sharpest case in a single session, since machine attention is a demonstrable operation that runs with no awareness at all.

Sessions 2 to 6 each close with a self-assessed activity — five in total. Session 7 has none: it is given over to the final synthesis report, due Friday 16 October.

A weekly learning cycle

  1. Read the overview and identify the session’s key concept (Session 4 has two).
  2. Complete the core readings; use the further readings to extend a particular interest.
  3. Begin the activity with the supplied machine-human chat prompt.
  4. Produce the weekly artifact and answer the reflection questions.
  5. Score your work using the four-point self-assessment scale and name one next step.

Self-assessment scale

  • 4 — Strong: clear, accurate, well-supported, and independently developed.
  • 3 — Proficient: mostly clear and accurate, with relevant support.
  • 2 — Developing: partial understanding or uneven support.
  • 1 — Beginning: minimal evidence of the criterion.
  • 0 — Not yet shown: missing or not attempted.
