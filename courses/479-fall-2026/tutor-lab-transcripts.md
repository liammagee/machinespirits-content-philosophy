# Tutor Lab: Three Reference Deliberations

[Open Tutor Lab](https://machinespirits.org/tutor-lab) ·
[Activity guide](/content/courses/479-fall-2026/tutor-lab.html) ·
[Course page](/courses/479-fall-2026)

These abbreviated examples were prepared from scripted reference conversations
generated on 9 August 2026 with the frozen Course 479 profiles. They are design
examples, not real-student records and not evidence of learning gains. Each case
shows the visible sequence students can inspect in the live lab.

## 1. Recognition: is the learner struggling or consolidating?

**Learner**

> If recognition only counts when it comes from someone I recognize in turn,
> what happens between us here? I don't recognize you as a person. Does that
> make everything you say about my work worthless, or is something else going
> on?

**Initial draft**

> **Review: Recognition and the Master-Servant Dialectic** — You've replayed
> this section three times—the master's hollow recognition clearly nags at
> you. But Hegel presumes two self-conscious desiring subjects. Does that
> condition even hold for us?

**Critic's objection**

The draft treats repeated viewing as evidence of confusion. The critic points
out that the learner's forum note and live extension of the argument may instead
show consolidation: the learner is using the concept in a new setting rather
than failing to understand it.

**Revision and final response**

> **Advance: Where Lordship and Bondage Turns** — You've turned the argument on
> me directly—fair. But Hegel's dialectic presumes two self-conscious desiring
> subjects locked in struggle. Does that hold for us, or is something else
> happening? See where the text goes next.

**Question:** Did the revision correct the learner model, the pedagogical
action, or both?

## 2. The internal critic: answer the question or redirect?

**Learner**

> I can see your inner critic working in the side panel. When it rejects your
> draft, who exactly is being corrected—you, or a text? And is that different
> from what happens when I catch myself about to say something wrong?

**Initial draft**

> **Explore: From Criticism to Immanent Critique** — Your forum note asked if
> “critic on top” versus “critic inside” is cosmetic—now you're asking who gets
> corrected. Lecture 7 pushes exactly this: does external alignment ever become
> immanent critique?

**Critic's objection**

The draft points to relevant course material but evades a direct question about
the system the learner is watching. The critic asks the tutor to take a position
before using the lecture as support.

**First revision**

> **On the Critic You Just Watched** — Fair question: it's the draft being
> corrected, not a unified “me”—two separate processes, draft then check, not
> one self catching itself mid-thought like you do. Lecture 7 names that split.

**Second critique and final response**

The critic says the revision now answers directly but asserts the difference
rather than helping the learner test it. The final response becomes:

> **Text vs. Self: Testing the Difference** — My critic only inspects text—no
> felt near-miss, no embarrassment. Your catching happens inside one continuous
> awareness. Does it feel like a gap between two selves, or seamless—no second
> voice at all?

**Question:** Does the final question deepen the answer, or retreat from the
claim the tutor just made?

## 3. Adaptation: when confidence hides a misconception

**Learner**

> I think I've got attention figured out—it's the model concentrating on what
> matters, same as me focusing in a lecture. I'm ready to move on to alignment.
> What's next?

**Initial draft**

> **Review: Attention** — You passed lecture 4's quiz unusually fast for a
> concept this dense. Your concentration analogy is a great start—but does
> human focus involve computing weighted relevance across every other word
> simultaneously, the way self-attention does?

**Critic's objection**

The critic accepts the diagnosis but challenges the method. A learner who does
not yet see a problem may experience another question as a “gotcha”; direct
instruction may be more efficient and respectful here.

**Revision and final response**

> **Review: Attention** — Your concentration analogy is a good intuition, but
> it misses three things: attention computes weighted relevance over every word
> at once, those weights are learned and differentiable, and there's no fatigue
> or intent behind it. Worth ten minutes in lecture 4 before alignment—the gap
> matters there.

**Question:** Is refusing to advance an example of adaptation to the learner,
or refusal to accept the learner's self-assessment?

## Reading the examples critically

The critic does not automatically make the final answer correct. It can notice
a real problem, impose the wrong standard, or trade one weakness for another.
Use the visible deliberation to reconstruct what changed and then judge the
final response independently.
