A mix of theory and practice

How do you build a sense of your field and its gaps? This course aims to make sense of a term – academic literature – at once mundane and mystical. It starts with fundamentals: how to build, annotate, analyze, map and translate a literature database into a literature review. It progresses to think about how AI and other technology can help foster more critical, analytic and reflexive modes of interpretation and understandings of the field.

Anatomy of a journal article

What is a journal article?

Think back to Weber: the vehicle of choice for transmitting Wissenschaft, or knowledge

We read dozens, hundreds, thousands…

Our life is conditioned by their results: “AI” today rests on “Attention is All You Need” (200K+ citations)

How do you analyze one? How do you write one?

A quick history

The journal is a recent invention

Articles

The collection that makes an issue

Authors

Who write each article

An editor

Who reviews and assembles the articles

Peer reviewers

Other scholars the editor invites to review

A publisher

Recruits the editor; typesets, sells and distributes

A readership

Subscribers, very often university libraries

A convenient way to communicate science, 17th–19th centuries

Aside · the perversity of publishing

Libraries pay for articles their own staff wrote, edited and reviewed. Why?

An alternative

Open access

But who sustains the volunteer labour, the reputation and the reach?

A side effect

Predatory publishing

And the article processing charge, which can run to thousands of dollars

What is the purpose of an article?

An article makes an argument.

It is persuasive writing: it aims to change a reader’s mind, and it has to use evidence to do it. Every part of an article serves that aim.

The review’s own research question

What’s the gap, and how will you show it?

Argue

That the gap exists. It should eventually coincide with your research question.

Search

Choose search terms; AI can translate yours into the ones you don’t know. Read the references of a few good articles.

Organize

By theme: what are the themes? By time: how have debates evolved? Then an introduction and conclusion.

Metaphors of knowledge

Talk your reader through the field to the gap

Up and down

General to specific

“More generally…”, “Theoretically…”, “More specifically…”, “Locally…”

Side to side

Similar to different

“Similarly…”, “Along the same lines…”, “However…”, “In contrast…”

Control and drama

Steer the path

“This review will cover…”, “Meanwhile…”, “Despite…”, “This review has covered…”

Work backwards: find the gap first, then organize the steps that lead to it.

Work it like a puzzle

Whole to part, part to whole

Understand the whole

Does the whole picture make sense?

Interpret the path

Fill it in piece by piece, then go back

More vocabulary: the hermeneutic circle. Understand the whole in order to interpret the path; interpret the path in order to understand the whole.

1

4

No persuasive purpose

5

A laundry list of gaps: narrow down to the one or two you’re likely to follow

Checklist

1

What are the key terms of the (draft) research question? List them…

2

What are the norms of your field? And what is your field?

3

What is a reasonable method or recipe for your literature review?

4

What are related terms (can AI find reasonable synonyms)?

5

What do you get when you search Google Scholar / library databases for these terms?

6

Do you have existing articles / authors to draw from?

7

What citation system are you using? Can you code / organize literature in the way you’d like?

8

What is the best organization of the literature? Thematic, field (for multi-/interdisciplinary work), chronological, spatial, general / special?

9

Are there classics you are expected to cite? References with a very large number of citations (e.g. Braun & Clarke 2006; Vaswani et al. 2017; Bender et al. 2021)?

Do some sketches · a “fast” review

Time, then theme, then space

Criticism of AI dates back to the inception of the field itself. For example, Dreyfus (1965) discussed the brittleness of (etc)… By the early 1980s, scepticism voiced by philosophers such as Searle (1980) in his famous “Chinese room” experiment, contributed to the emergence of an AI winter. More recently, as generative AI has largely revived the field, criticism has been directed toward both issues of social justice and bias (e.g. Bender et al. 2021) and potential existential risk (e.g. lesswrong blog). In some cases, critical AI scholars have also argued the existential case is a convenient disguise that obscures more immediate negative social effects (Gebru and Torres 2024). Moreover scholars from outside the US – particularly in the Global South – have begun to argue AI constitutes a form of neocolonialism, impacting digital sovereignty (Keerthiraj, & Misra 2025)

Just be approximate. Works with or without AI. Don’t commit to this structure just yet!

Then develop a plan

Light, non-committal, ready to cut and paste

1

Tentative gap: what do you think it is?

2

Scaffold the review: how many sections? Subsections? Add preliminary citations

3

Key transitions: what’s the drama in the review? “Also argued”, “moreover”…

4

Stay light: be prepared to cut and paste

5

Qualify the gap as the literature grows: add adjectives to narrow it. Then back to step 1

How was Gap Finder made?

As with the class constellation, it was built with Claude over several conversations. This prompt is a starting point for a similar game:

Build a single-file interactive HTML game for a graduate seminar on literature reviews that shows how a research question and its literature shape each other. Embed a small field of real article records (title, year, authors, venue, DOI, a line from the abstract) taken from OpenAlex. The student builds a question by adding or removing concepts (topic, who, which practice, which issue, where), or types their own question and the game picks out its concepts. Score each paper by how many of the question’s concepts appear in its title or abstract. Show the field in 3D with three.js: papers matching every concept gather at the centre, papers matching all but one orbit nearby, and the rest drift outward, moving each time the question changes. The goal is to refine the question until only 1–3 papers answer it directly while many sit nearby. Record each change as a version on a visible path, and when the goal is met, list the papers to read and draft a signposted gap statement.

It can only search what is embedded in it: a claude.ai page cannot call OpenAlex live · Iterate one change at a time · Check every paper against the source

3 · Create a short plan / recipe method:

  • “Search for these terms / filters”
  • “Create a database of 10 articles”
  • “Scan the articles for relevance - discard those that are not relevant”
  • “With remaining articles: review references, search for more recent citations”
  • “Once I have 50 articles: code / annotate for relevance”
  • “Map out review structure; add citations, continue to search under subheadings”
