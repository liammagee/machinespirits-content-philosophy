## Concrete task specifications for review

The following are new proposals, not settled requirements. Canvas assignment drafts identify them as such. References are additional to proposed word counts; submit accessible documents, with maps also represented in text. A concise AI-use note should describe consequential assistance or state that none was used.

| Task | Proposed deliverable |
|---|---|
| 1 | 400–600 words and 3–5 initial scholarly sources; explain relevance, significance, feasibility, and an unresolved question |
| 2 | 500–700 words and a search record covering databases/catalogs, terms, dates, and selection decisions |
| 3 | One map, equivalent text outline, and 400–600 words; connect approximately 6–8 relevant sources and justify relationships |
| 4 | One 600–800-word synthesis section connecting at least three sources, plus 150–200 words explaining audience, voice, and evidence |

The instructor must select the approach. Source adequacy should follow the question and argument, not citation padding; any minimum source count needs approval.

## Proposed rubrics

These allocations have not been installed as Canvas grading rubrics. Each task totals 100 points.

| Task | Allocation and evidence to assess |
|---|---|
| 1 | Question and significance 30; feasibility and scope 25; engagement with initial sources 25; clarity and process explanation 20 |
| 2 | Fit between purpose, audience, and scope 25; transparent search and selection 35; limits and justification 25; presentation and traceability 15 |
| 3 | Meaningful relationships and organizing principle 35; source support 30; gaps and uncertainties 20; readable visual/text explanation 15 |
| 4 | Synthesis across sources 35; interpretive accuracy and evidence 30; audience and scholarly voice 20; explanatory commentary and disclosure 15 |
| 5 | Organizing argument and outline 30; developed synthesis 30; transitions and signposting 25; justified structural revision 15 |
| 6 | Specific, candid account of methods or AI use 30; verification and researcher responsibility 30; positionality connected to an actual choice 25; clarity and relevant evidence 15 |
| Final | Question, scope, and contribution 20; sources and traceability 20; interpretation and synthesis 25; structure and scholarly voice 15; reflexivity and AI disclosure 10; evidence of revision 10 |

Common performance descriptors, applied to the task's evidence:

- **Strong:** clear, defensible decisions; relevant sources are used accurately; relationships are argued, not merely listed; uncertainties and revisions are explained with examples.
- **Sound:** the task is substantially fulfilled and supported; interpretation is mostly coherent; some relationships, limits, or revisions need fuller justification.
- **Developing:** a recognizable project is present, but important choices are asserted rather than supported; the work leans on summary, weak connections, or incomplete traceability.
- **Limited evidence:** essential parts are missing or cannot be evaluated from the submission; unsupported claims or unexplained methods prevent a defensible judgment.

These are descriptors, not an automatic letter-grade mapping. Provide criterion-level feedback. Handle suspected integrity violations through University procedures rather than treating a rubric score as an integrity finding.

7. **Recording:** choose whether sessions will be recorded, where students access recordings, consent arrangements, and retention. Do not copy these rules from another course.
