Common pitfalls

Five ways a review goes wrong

1

An annotated bibliography in place of a literature review

2

A tone that ignores the “two cultures” (Snow, 1959)

3

No signposting, metadiscourse or discourse markers

4

No persuasive purpose

5

A laundry list of gaps: narrow down to the one or two you’re likely to follow

Common pitfalls. An annotated bibliography lists and summarizes sources one by one; a literature review synthesizes them into an argument. Tone: navigating C. P. Snow’s two cultures (next slide, and a reading for next week). Missing signposting or metadiscourse. Missing persuasive purpose. And a laundry list of gaps, when you need to narrow to the one or two you are likely to follow.
