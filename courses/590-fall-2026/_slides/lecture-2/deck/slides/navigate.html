Metaphors of knowledge

Talk your reader through the field to the gap

Up and down

General to specific

“More generally…”, “Theoretically…”, “More specifically…”, “Locally…”

Side to side

Similar to different

“Similarly…”, “Along the same lines…”, “However…”, “In contrast…”

Control and drama

Steer the path

“This review will cover…”, “Meanwhile…”, “Despite…”, “This review has covered…”

Work backwards: find the gap first, then organize the steps that lead to it.

The field is a space you walk the reader through; the gap is where you take them. Use the language of argument, or metadiscourse, to join the dots. Moving up and down: “More generally…”, “At an abstract level…”, “More specifically…”, “At a concrete level…”. Moving side to side: “Similarly…”, “Along the same lines…”, “However…”, “Conversely…”, “On the other hand…”. Controlling the path: “This review will cover…”, “The literature describes several facets of…”, and some drama: “Meanwhile…”, “Despite…”, “X has disputed Y’s findings…”.
