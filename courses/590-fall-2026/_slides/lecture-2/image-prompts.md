# Lecture 2: image prompts for ChatGPT

One prompt per slide, named by the slide's heading in `lecture-2.md`. Paste the **house style** line at the end of
each prompt so the set looks like one family. Ask for a **square image (1024×1024)**: on the slides, images sit in
a column beside the text.

**House style (append to every prompt):** Contemporary editorial illustration, the kind used for a long-form
magazine essay: confident composition, a limited palette of deep ink-navy, warm off-white and one ember-orange
accent, subtle paper grain, soft directional light. No text, letters, logos or captions anywhere in the image.
Square, 1:1.

Image generators still garble words, which is why every prompt says "no text". If a word appears anyway,
regenerate rather than fix it.

## Quick history (1): the salon as peer review (deliberately anachronistic)

> An oil painting in the manner of a late-18th-century French conversation piece: a candlelit Parisian salon,
> around 1760, with gilded boiserie, tall windows with heavy silk curtains, a harpsichord in the corner and a
> crowded oval table. Powdered wigs, silk waistcoats, lace cuffs, a hostess presiding from an armchair. The
> company is gathered to judge a manuscript: one guest reads aloud from loose handwritten pages while the others
> lean in, frown, gesture and whisper, as if this were peer review conducted as polite conversation. The
> anachronism, painted entirely in period technique as if the artist took it for granted: the guest of honour
> whose work is being judged is a tall automaton of polished brass and porcelain, in a powdered wig and an
> embroidered coat, with a softly glowing glass panel for a face; the manuscript pages it has produced pour from
> a slot in its chest; a footman carries in a tray of punched cards like a dish of sweets; a lady consults a
> small glowing pane in her lap as if it were a fan. Nobody in the room finds any of this strange. Rich
> chiaroscuro, varnished surface, fine craquelure, a gilt frame just visible at the edges. Composition and
> lighting in the tradition of Lemonnier's *An Evening at Madame Geoffrin's*.

(For this one, drop the house style's palette and "editorial illustration" phrases: keep only "No text,
letters, logos or captions anywhere in the image. Square, 1:1.")

## Anatomy of a Journal Article

> A journal article shown as an anatomical specimen: a printed academic paper laid open on a dissection table
> and pinned like a biological plate, its sections (title block, abstract, introduction, literature review,
> methods, findings, conclusion, references) peeled back in layers to reveal a skeleton of arrows and argument
> underneath. Clean overhead view, precise and slightly surreal.

## Aside: Perversity of Publishing

> A circular economy drawn as an impossible machine: university scholars feed manuscripts into one end of a
> gleaming publishing press; reviewers and editors, also scholars, tend its gears without pay; out of the other
> end comes a bound journal that a university library buys back through a coin slot. The loop is visibly closed
> and slightly absurd, in the spirit of a Rube Goldberg diagram.

## What is the purpose of an article?

> Persuasion as a physical act: a single reader seated in an armchair, and a sequence of carefully placed
> stepping stones made of stacked papers and charts leading across a dark river towards them. Each stone is a
> piece of evidence; the path is the argument. Calm, quiet, the reader just beginning to lean forward.

## Typical structure of an Article

> A modern building in cross-section, each storey a section of a research article: a bright entrance lobby
> (introduction), a library floor (literature review), a workshop with instruments (methodology), a gallery of
> findings, an observation deck at the top (conclusion), and an archive in the basement (references). One
> staircase runs through all floors, suggesting a single argument connecting them.

## Learn from Marx?

> Two journeys on one diagram, drawn as a mountain: on the left, a climber ascends from a busy, concrete
> street-level world of objects and people up into the clean, cold air of abstract concepts at the summit; on the
> right, a guide descends the other face from the summit back into the street, now lit up and legible. The
> ascent is analysis; the descent is presentation. Elegant, diagrammatic, quietly witty.

## Google Gemini's view (a ChatGPT counterpart, for comparison)

Use the slide's own words as the prompt, unchanged, so students can compare the two systems' answers:

> create an image showing how a literature review is a mini-argument embedded within the larger argument of an
> article or dissertation

(Leave off the house style for this one: the point is to see what the system does unprompted.)

## Hour 2: Mapping Fields and Minding Gaps

> An aerial view of a vast field at dusk, laid out like a map: thousands of small glowing points (articles)
> clustered into dense towns and thinning into countryside, with faint roads between them. In one place the
> lights stop: a dark, unclaimed clearing at the meeting point of three busy regions. A single walker with a
> lantern stands at its edge.

## Metaphors of Knowledge: Navigating fields and finding gaps

> A hiker's path across rolling terrain, seen from above, with the path rising to hilltops (more generally) and
> dropping into valleys (more specifically), crossing bridges to neighbouring hills (similarly) and doubling back
> at forks (however). Small signposts along the way are blank wooden arrows, no writing. The path ends at a gap
> in a hedge.

## Work it like a Puzzle

> A large jigsaw puzzle half-assembled on a table, viewed from above: the completed edge and a few clusters in
> place, the picture they form is a map of a research field; a hand holds one piece over the gap it fits, while
> the box lid showing the whole picture leans against a stack of books. A visual hermeneutic circle: part and
> whole at once.

## Work it like a Puzzle: the hermeneutic circle

> The hermeneutic circle as a place: a reader sits at the centre of a round, domed reading room whose curved
> wall is one continuous manuscript, its lines running all the way around the room and back to where they began.
> In her hands she holds a single torn fragment of that same manuscript, and she is looking up from it to the
> wall. A thin thread of warm light runs from the fragment out to its place in the wall, travels the whole
> circumference, and returns to the page in her hands, so part and whole keep illuminating each other. Quiet,
> contemplative, seen from slightly above, the circle of the room clearly legible.

## Shark Tank?: the literature review as a pitch to investors

> A graduate researcher stands alone on the polished floor of a dark, theatrical pitch studio, presenting to a
> row of five investors in deep leather armchairs. Instead of a product on a plinth, the researcher's display is
> a model of the "market" of ideas: a miniature trading floor of bookstalls and journal stacks, crowded with
> traders, crates of bound volumes and busy little shopfronts, except for one small, empty, lit-up plot in
> the middle where nobody is selling anything yet. The researcher points at that empty plot. The investors
> lean forward: one peers through reading glasses at the gap, one taps a pen against a stack of offprints,
> one is unconvinced, arms folded. Spotlights from above, the studio behind falling away into darkness. The
> mood is sceptical, high-stakes and slightly comic, as if a literature review were a business pitch. The empty
> plot is the one ember-orange accent in the picture. No price tags, no signage and no television branding.

## More on tone...

> Two writing desks facing each other across a narrow room: one a spotless laboratory bench with instruments,
> graph paper and a cool blue lamp; the other a cluttered humanities desk with open books, marginal notes and a
> warm amber lamp. The two writers, seen from behind, are passing a single sheet of paper across the gap.

## First: What do we do when we open an article?

> A single academic article shown as a book opened in several ways at once: one hand reads it from the top,
> another flips straight to the last page, a third runs a finger down the references, a fourth holds a
> magnifying glass over the methods. Four hands, one document, a playful overhead still life.

## Hour 3: Designing a Literature Review

> An architect's drafting table at night: a blueprint for a building whose floor plan is made of interlocking
> stacks of papers and books, with tracing paper, a scale ruler and a mug of coffee. The plan is still being
> drawn: some walls are firm ink, others pencil.

## Growing your database

> A single glowing document at the centre of a dark space with three trails of light leaving it: one backwards
> into older, yellowed books (its references), one forwards towards newer, brighter pages (the work that cites
> it), and one sideways along a shelf of journal issues from the same series. Like a constellation being drawn
> in real time.

## Also consider...

> Heteroscopia, "many visions": one landscape seen through many different lenses at once: a camera eye, a
> telescope, a stained-glass window, a phone screen, a pair of reading glasses and a scanning sensor grid, each
> lens showing the same scene differently, arranged as a collage on a table.
