# Lecture 3: image prompts for ChatGPT

One prompt per slide, named by the slide's heading in `lecture-3.md`. Paste the **house style** line at the end of
each prompt so the set looks like one family. Ask for a **square image (1024×1024)**: on the slides, images sit in
a column beside the text.

**House style (append to every prompt):** Contemporary editorial illustration, the kind used for a long-form
magazine essay: confident composition, a limited palette of deep ink-navy, warm off-white and one ember-orange
accent, subtle paper grain, soft directional light. No text, letters, logos or captions anywhere in the image.
Square, 1:1.

Image generators still garble words, which is why every prompt says "no text". If a word appears anyway,
regenerate rather than fix it.

## Sharing Task 2: Could Someone Else Bake Your Review?

> A literature review as a recipe being handed between two cooks: one passes a handwritten recipe card across a
> kitchen counter to another, who stands before a row of mixing bowls filled with stacked papers, index cards and
> journal pages instead of flour and eggs. On the counter, a cake in progress built from layers of books. The
> second cook squints at the card, unsure of one step. Warm, domestic, a little comic.

## What is in a 'Vibe'?

> A scholar at a desk late at night, half-lit by a reading lamp and half by the soft glow of a screen, surrounded
> by drifting loose pages that seem to float on a gentle current around them. Some pages settle into a neat
> pattern on the desk, others swirl away. The mood is unhurried and absorbed: luxuriating in a text rather than
> racing through it.

## This Week's Readings: Moretti and Snow

> Two ways of seeing one library. On the left half, a reader with a magnifying glass bent close over a single open
> book, every line visible. On the right half, the same library seen from high above as a landscape: thousands of
> books arranged into rivers, clusters and mountain ranges, like a map seen from an aircraft. A thin seam where
> the two halves meet suggests two cultures sharing one building.

## Topic 1: Opening an article

> A printed journal article opening like a door: the first page swings inward on hinges, and through the gap a
> reader glimpses corridors of argument, footnotes and figures stretching away inside. The reader stands on the
> threshold, one hand on the page-door, deciding where to step first.

## "Summarize This"

> One long scroll of dense text fed into a small elegant press, which squeezes it into a single bright droplet of
> ink caught in a glass dish. Beside the press, a second dish holds a few carefully cut-out fragments of the
> original scroll, pinned like specimens: two ways of making the long thing short.

## "Analyze This" - Close Reading

> A reader and a small, quiet, glowing companion (an abstract presence, not a robot) sitting side by side at a
> long table, moving together along a single line of text that runs the length of the table like a road. The
> reader points at one word; the companion leans in to look. Intimate, patient, line-by-line.

## Annotating a Difficult Passage

> A labyrinth drawn from lines of text, seen from above: the corridors are sentences, the dead ends are
> sub-clauses, and a single reader with a lantern works through it, leaving a trail of handwritten marginal notes
> pinned to the walls behind them. The centre of the maze glows faintly. Difficult but navigable.

## Topic 2: Finding literature

> A researcher with a lantern in a vast, dim archive whose shelves stretch beyond sight. Some books on the
> shelves glow softly, as if answering the lantern, forming a faint trail through the stacks; others stay dark.
> One glowing book on a high shelf is a decoy: its light is a little too perfect.

## Build a quick library

> A single journal article lying open on a desk, its reference list unspooling off the page like threads, each
> thread pulling a new book or paper out of the air and onto a growing pile beside it. One thread ends in nothing:
> a book that dissolves into mist as it is pulled, a hint that not every citation is real.

## Topic 3: Start a conceptual map

> A field of scholarship drawn as a constellation: papers as stars of different sizes, linked by fine lines into
> clusters, with a scholar on a hilltop at night tracing the shapes with an outstretched finger. Between two
> bright clusters, a conspicuously dark patch of sky where a star should be.

## Maps and Gaps in Practice

> A cartographer's table covered by a hand-drawn map of an unfamiliar territory: rivers, towns and roads drawn in
> fine ink, with one region left blank and marked only by a small compass rose and a question of white space.
> The cartographer's hand hovers over the blank area holding a pen, about to decide what belongs there.

## Start an AI-Use Record

> A researcher's open logbook on a desk, in the tradition of a ship's log or a lab notebook: neat columns of
> handwritten entries, small sketches, ticks and crossings-out, and a few pages clipped in from elsewhere. Beside
> it, a softly glowing screen. The logbook is clearly the record of a working partnership, kept honestly.
