# Lecture 3 (final): image prompts for ChatGPT

One prompt per slide, named by the slide's heading in `lecture-3-final.md`. Paste the **house style** line at the
end of each prompt so the set looks like one family. Ask for a **square image (1024×1024)**: on the slides, images
sit in a column beside the text. Three prompts (Sharing Task 2, Moretti and Snow, AI-Use Record) are carried over
from `_slides/lecture-3/image-prompts.md`.

**House style (append to every prompt):** Contemporary editorial illustration, the kind used for a long-form
magazine essay: confident composition, a limited palette of deep ink-navy, warm off-white and one ember-orange
accent, subtle paper grain, soft directional light. No text, letters, logos or captions anywhere in the image.
Square, 1:1.

Image generators still garble words, which is why every prompt says "no text". If a word appears anyway,
regenerate rather than fix it.

## Sharing Task 2: Could Someone Else Bake Your Review?

> A literature review as a recipe being handed between two cooks: one passes a handwritten recipe card across a
> kitchen counter to another, who stands before a row of mixing bowls filled with stacked papers, index cards and
> journal pages instead of flour and eggs. On the counter, a cake in progress built from layers of books. The
> second cook squints at the card, unsure of one step. Warm, domestic, a little comic.

## This Week's Readings: Moretti and Snow

> Two ways of seeing one library. On the left half, a reader with a magnifying glass bent close over a single open
> book, every line visible. On the right half, the same library seen from high above as a landscape: thousands of
> books arranged into rivers, clusters and mountain ranges, like a map seen from an aircraft. A thin seam where
> the two halves meet suggests two cultures sharing one building.

## Analyzing PDFs?

> A stack of journal articles on a desk, each bound with a ribbon and a small wax seal, beside an open laptop. One
> article has had its seal lifted and slides freely toward the laptop; the others stay sealed. In the corner, a
> small desktop computer glows quietly under the desk with its own lamp, working on a page offline, no wires
> leading out of the room. A careful, slightly legal mood.

## Seed articles - The Two Cultures and the Problem of Alignment

> One large compass needle at the centre of the image, trying to settle. Two figures stand on either side holding
> it steady by threads: on the left, a scholar surrounded by open books and annotated pages, reading; on the right,
> a researcher with a clipboard, gauges and a row of measuring instruments, testing. The needle points between
> them, not quite still. A shared question approached from two cultures.

## 'Fast' Reading / 'Vibe' Scholarship: Two groups

> Two small reading groups seen from above at two round tables, each huddled over the same kind of paper but
> sitting in different arrangements: one group reading from the outside edges inward, one starting at the top, one
> pair diving into the middle. Dotted paths trace each reader's route across their pages. Lively, collaborative,
> slightly hurried.

## First paper: Speed Group Reading, without AI (15 minutes)

> Three readers working on one long printed article laid out across a table: one holds a magnifying glass over the
> title and conclusion at the edges, one reads from the top down with a finger tracing the lines, one has lifted a
> middle page to inspect a diagram of the method. Pencils, sticky notes, no screens anywhere. Focused and
> companionable.

## Second paper: Speed *Augmented* Reading (15 minutes)

> The same three readers and the same long printed article, but now a soft orange light from a small floating
> lamp-like assistant hovers over the pages, casting highlighted paths across the text. Some highlights land on
> exactly the right passage; one beam lights up an empty margin where nothing is written. The readers compare the
> lit passages against their own pencil marks.

## From the Article (close reading) to the Conversation (distant reading)

> A camera pulling back: in the foreground, a single open article seen in close detail on a desk; behind it, the
> desk becomes a city of stacked papers seen from a rising viewpoint, and further back still, the whole city
> becomes a pattern of lights and streets, like a field of literature seen from altitude. One continuous zoom from
> page to landscape.

## Strategies for literature mapping

> Four small panels of the same set of index cards pinned to a wall, arranged four different ways: in a long line
> like a timeline; in clustered islands by theme; joined by threads in a web of relationships; and pinned onto a
> faint world map by place. The same cards each time, four different orders. Clear, diagrammatic, elegant.

## Generate and visualize the mapping

> A researcher at a drafting table watching a rough pencil sketch of linked boxes transform, across the surface of
> the table, into a clean, glowing diagram of nodes and connecting lines that lifts slightly off the page. One
> region of the diagram is conspicuously empty, outlined with a dashed line: the gap. The researcher holds a pencil
> ready to question it.

## Break!

> An empty seminar room at dusk, chairs pushed back from a long table scattered with papers, coffee cups and an
> open laptop, steam still rising from one cup. Through the window, the evening sky. Calm, a pause between two
> halves of a long evening.

## Start an AI-Use Record

> A researcher's open logbook on a desk, in the tradition of a ship's log or a lab notebook: neat columns of
> handwritten entries, small sketches, ticks and crossings-out, and a few pages clipped in from elsewhere. Beside
> it, a softly glowing screen. The logbook is clearly the record of a working partnership, kept honestly.
