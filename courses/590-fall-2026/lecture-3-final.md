## Prompt samples - for people as well as machines!


Strategic prompt: "Work outside in. Start with the abstract, introduction, references, and conclusion. State your provisional interpretation, then identify the specific additional sections that would most seriously test it. Read those sections and revise the interpretation, citing passages. Use references to situate the article."

Sequential prompt: "Work top to bottom. Read the supplied text in its presented order. At each section, record how the central argument develops, which evidence is added, and what remains unresolved. Cite passages. Do not fill in missing sections."

Methodological prompt: "Work inside out. Begin with the methodology. Explain what that section establishes, then trace backward and forward to reconstruct the article’s larger argument. Distinguish what you verified from what you inferred."

## Strategies for literature discovery 

Traditional:

 - Keyword search (find the keywords in the respective articles; search Google Scholar, library databases)
 - Author search (have these authors written collectively or individually on this topic?)
 - What does this article reference? (mine the articles' references)
 - What has referenced this article? (find what has cited these articles in Google Scholar)
 - Journal / issue navigation (what else is published in this venue? and what are other journals commonly cited by this article?)
 - Other?
 
Augmented:
 - AI as exploration / discovery: make up queries:
   - "Anthropic researchers authored this article. What research have other companies published on alignment?"
   - "Birhane has published a lot on critical AI. Has she written elsewhere on alignment?"
 - Connect to your own expertise: "I've been reading about how organizational leadership is responding to AI. Have there been studies of how leadership addresses alignment?"

## Strategies for literature mapping 

Consider the organizational approaches we discussed last week:

 - Chronological (this literature follows from this other literature)
 - Thematic / conceptual (this literature discusses this theme; this other literature discusses this other theme - and this is where they overlap / differ)
 - Relational (this literature relates / responds to this other literature)
 - Spatial (this literature studies phenomenon X in the US; this literature studies phenomenon X in South Africa)
 
Can we start to make sense of our emergent database via one or more of these approaches?
